Far from Earth, “normal” is not enough

  • The doctor is minutes away: up to about 22 minutes one way between Earth and Mars
  • Space changes normal: in-flight hematocrit 10–12 % higher, resting core temperature about 1.0 °C higher, sleep about 6.1 h of 8.5 h scheduled
  • Population ranges miss the individual: a change can be large for one person and still “in range”
  • Monitoring is periodic: discrete devices, vital signs mainly during exercise and spacewalks

Every reading judged three ways

  • Personal: unusual for this astronaut? (robust baseline, z-score, CuSum)
  • Space: expected or concerning in this mission? (NASA standards and evidence)
  • Earth: would a clinic flag it? (clinical reference ranges) · plus Quality: can we trust the reading?
  • Example, Mars transit day 143: K. Osei’s resting pulse 69 bpm, her usual 58 → Personal Act, Space Nominal, Earth Nominal: caught only by personal baseline

ARGUS

Measure with what’s on board

  • Camera pulse: 30 s with the laptop webcam, POS method, on-device face tracking, quality score from five checks; video never leaves the device
  • Reaction Self-Test: 3 minutes, modeled on NASA’s PVT-B; lapses compared with the astronaut’s own pre-flight runs
  • Vision check (screening only): Amsler grid per eye and near acuity on the card-calibrated laptop screen, modeled on the ISS laptop tests; a new marked area since the last test → Watch, near the centre → Act
  • Guided entry: blood pressure, SpO₂, temperature, glucose, mass, sleep, mood, symptoms, each with “how to measure”
  • Face fluid-shift scan (experimental, not validated): 3 s of face geometry vs your own first scan; trend only
  • Camera pulse not yet compared with a reference device: shown with a quality score, used for screening and trends.

Space weather, dose and cabin air

  • NOAA GOES proton flux: live, else cached, else bundled snapshot, always with its age → NOAA S-scale
  • Crew dose ledger vs NASA-STD-3001: career < 600 mSv, solar-event design limit < 250 mSv; Mars cruise 1.8 mSv/day measured by Curiosity RAD
  • Storm replay (synthetic) S2-class profile → shelter advisory (demo policy), modeled event dose labeled illustrative
  • Cabin CO₂ vs the 3 mmHg 1-hour limit and the 2.5 mmHg 7-day headache line
  • NASA DONKI event list: not connected in this build (service unreachable on build day)
