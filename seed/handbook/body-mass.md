## What it is

Your body mass in kilograms, compared with your pre-flight mass.

## Why it matters in space

Losing mass in flight can mean you are not eating enough; NASA plans an average of about 3,035 kcal per crewmember per day [SRC_STD3001_ECLSS]. Inadequate food and nutrition is a NASA human-research risk [SRC_NASA_RISKS].

## How to measure

- Use the mass-measurement device per its procedure. [demo wording]
- Measure at the same time of day. [demo wording]

## How ARGUS judges it

- **Earth:** Watch for a loss of 5 % or more against pre-flight within 30 days; Act for 10 % or more [SRC_DEMO_RULE_BANDS].
- **Space:** the same bands [SRC_DEMO_RULE_BANDS]; the space context is the planned food intake [SRC_STD3001_ECLSS].
- **Personal:** your reading is compared with your own usual, for drops; ARGUS never assumes a day-to-day spread smaller than 0.5 kg [SRC_DEMO_BASELINE_ENGINE].

## What to do

- **Watch:** "Re-measure. If it is still outside your usual range, tell the CMO." [demo wording]
- **Act:** "Tell the CMO now and send a report to the flight surgeon." [demo wording]
